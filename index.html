📝 Grammar — Narrative Tenses
How to tell a story in English like a native speaker.
⚙️ The Three Narrative Tenses
Past Simple
✅ Completed actions in the story. The main events, told in sequence.
"We booked the tickets on Friday. We arrived on Saturday morning."
Past Continuous
✅ Background actions / setting the scene. An ongoing action that gets interrupted.
"We were wandering around Montmartre when we stumbled upon a café."
Past Perfect
✅ Events that happened before the main story. Used to give background context or explain what had already occurred.
"I had never been to Paris before, so I was incredibly excited." / "By the time we arrived, we had already spent half our money on croissants."
💡 Key structure to remember: "We were doing X when Y happened." — This is one of the most natural, fluent-sounding constructions in spoken British English storytelling.
✏️ Fill in the Gaps

Use the correct tense of the verb in brackets. Write the full verb form.

1. We ______ around Montmartre when we ______ upon a little bookshop. Hint: background action interrupted by a sudden event → Past Continuous + Past Simple

🎙️ RP Pronunciation — The TRAP–BATH Split
One of the most distinctive features of British Received Pronunciation — and directly relevant to your Paris trip vocabulary!
📌 The Rule: In RP (Received Pronunciation), certain words with the letter A are pronounced with the long vowel /ɑː/ (as in "car"), not the short /æ/ (as in "cat"). This is sometimes called the TRAP–BATH split.

When does it apply? Before the sounds f, s, th (θ), and before n or m + another consonant — e.g. can't, last, France, after, chance, laugh, path, answer, dance.

💡 In American English (and some Northern British accents), ALL these words use the short /æ/. The long /ɑː/ is what marks RP.

🌟 Bonus: In your own words, explain the difference between spontaneous, impromptu, and impulsive. Are they interchangeable? Give an example for each.

This is a genuine B2+ vocabulary distinction — native speakers use these differently!

💡 Spontaneous vs Impromptu vs Impulsive:
Spontaneous — done naturally, without planning, but not necessarily recklessly. Often positive. "A spontaneous trip feels natural and freeing."
Impromptu — without preparation; often refers to performances, speeches, events. "An impromptu dinner party; an impromptu speech."
Impulsive — driven by sudden emotion, often without thinking of consequences. Can be negative. "An impulsive purchase you regret."
